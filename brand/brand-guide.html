01 · Idea

A drafting
table for rings.

Make-a-Ring is a design studio you can use without being a designer. The visual language comes from the jeweller's bench and the engineer's drawing: a grid, registration marks, dimension lines, title blocks, honest numbers. The ring is always the hero; the interface steps back.

Calm

One main action per view. Muted surfaces, one accent. Nothing blinks, bounces or shouts.

Precise

Real millimetres, real prices, real printability limits. Measurements are set in mono, like a drawing.

Hands-on

You touch the ring: drag it, scroll it, stretch its diameter. Every action answers at once.

Voice

Do

  • Talk to the person: "your ring", "pick a size".
  • Short sentences. Plain words. Verbs first on buttons: "Open the studio", "Try it on".
  • Numbers with units: "Ø 17.3 mm", "$78".
  • Say what happens next, and offer Undo after anything destructive.

Don't

  • Exclamation marks, hype, "amazing", emoji.
  • Jargon without help ("MSLA" always comes with "resin printer").
  • Section numbers or symbols as decoration (no §1).
  • Real brands, real people or invented testimonials.

02 · Logo

The mark

A ring seen from the front, with the diamond on top in the accent colour. The wordmark is Archivo ExtraBold, wide (118%), uppercase; the "-a-" takes the accent.

Rules

  • Clear space on every side: the height of the mark (dashed line above).
  • Smallest size: 20 px mark, 14 px wordmark.
  • The diamond always uses the theme accent; the ring and letters use ink.
  • In the app the logo is also the link home, with a "Home" button beside it.

Don't

  • Stretch, outline, rotate or add effects.
  • Recolour the diamond in anything but the accent.
  • Set the wordmark in another font or in lowercase.

04 · Typography

Three voices

Display · Archivo
Make a ring.

Landing: 800–900 weight, width 118–125 %, uppercase, tracking −0.03 em. App screens (studio, try on, review): 750 weight, width 112 %, sentence case, so long titles stay readable. Titles only; never for buttons or paragraphs.

Interface · Inter
Pick a size, then open the studio.

400 body, 500 chips, 600 buttons, tabs and card titles. Every control on the site is Inter. Body 15 px / 1.5.

Data · IBM Plex Mono
Ø 17.3 mm · US 7 · $78

Measurements, prices, small uppercase labels (11 px, 600, tracking 0.08 em), keyboard keys. Never for buttons or tabs.

Scale

11121314151620284470

Only these sizes (px). Small UI text is 12 or 13, never 11.5. Display sizes are fluid (clamp()) between 28 and 200.

Engraving fonts

Lora and Dancing Script (and the other engraving faces) are product content: they appear only in engraving previews and on the ring itself, never in the interface.

The curtain

Screens sit in a row. Between them a curtain (the page colour, the drafting grid and a thin accent edge) sweeps across in one continuous move: from the right when you go deeper, from the left when you go back. It speeds up as it covers the screen, the page swaps at the instant it is fully covered, and it carries straight on out the other side. The content itself never moves or fades, so text and images never flicker.

Home→Start→Size→Studio→Try on→Review
  • Nothing wobbles, bounces or loops for attention. The only idle motion is the ring turning.
  • With "reduce motion" switched on in the system, every transition is instant.

Sound

Quiet, synthesised, and always paired with something you can see. Muted from Settings; the choice is remembered.

07 · Layout

The ring first

Left: library

Patterns, your own patterns and templates. Click the active icon again to hide the panel.

Centre: the ring

The cross carousel around the 3D ring, the selectors, and the unrolled band.

Right: specs

Size, layout, band, material, engraving, printability, and the price with the next step.

  • The 3D ring is the bottom layer of the stage and is never cut off by a box.
  • On small or zoomed screens the interface scales down (to 80 %) so the ring keeps its room.
  • Colour theme, grid and sound toggles sit together in the top bar.
  • Every screen has a way home: the logo and the Home button.
